## ⚙️ For **Arduino Uno:**

### 🔹 **Normal ON/OFF (digital control)**

You can use **any digital pin** for this.
✅ Recommended pins:

```
2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 12, or 13
```

➡️ Example:

```cpp
#define LASER_PIN 7
```

and use `digitalWrite(LASER_PIN, HIGH/LOW)` for full ON/OFF control.

---

### 🔸 **Brightness Control (PWM mode)**

PWM = analog-style brightness via fast ON/OFF switching.
Only certain pins on the Uno support this.

✅ PWM-capable pins:

```
3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11
```

➡️ Example:

```cpp
#define LASER_PIN 9
```

and use `analogWrite(LASER_PIN, value);`
where `value` ranges from **0 (off)** to **255 (full brightness)**.

---

### ⚠️ **Note**

* The PWM pins usually have a **“~” (tilde symbol)** next to them on your Arduino board.
* Don’t use the same pin for both functions at once — just pick **one**:

  * Pin 7 → simple ON/OFF
  * Pin 9 → brightness control

How does the Switing on and off works . 
---

### 🌟 Think of it like a light switch

Your laser needs **electricity to flow** from the battery’s + (positive) to – (negative).
If the path is complete → **laser ON**
If the path is broken → **laser OFF**

---

### ⚡ What the MOSFET does

The **MOSFET in your D4184** is like an **automatic switch** placed on the **negative (–)** side of the laser.

So:

* The **+ (positive)** of the laser is *always connected* to power.
* The **– (negative)** wire of the laser goes *through* the MOSFET — it decides whether the laser can connect to ground or not.

---

### 🧠 When the Arduino sends a signal

| Arduino Signal        | What the D4184 does             | What happens                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **LOW (0V)**          | The switch (MOSFET) is **open** | No current can flow → ❌ Laser OFF |
| **HIGH (5V or 3.3V)** | The switch **closes**           | Current flows → ✅ Laser ON        |

---

### 💡 Simple flow of current

When **ON**:

```
+ (Power Bank → LM2596) → Laser → MOSFET (closed switch) → Ground
```

When **OFF**:

```
+ (Power Bank → LM2596) → Laser → (open switch ❌) → No Ground
```

No ground = no current = no light.

---

### 🎛️ The Arduino’s job

Your Arduino doesn’t power the laser directly.
It just sends a **tiny control signal** (like your finger flipping a switch) to the D4184 module.
The D4184 then handles the **actual laser current** safely.

---

### ⚙️ Summary in one line:

> Arduino tells the MOSFET when to connect the laser’s negative wire to ground.
> When connected → current flows → laser ON
> When disconnected → no current → laser OFF
